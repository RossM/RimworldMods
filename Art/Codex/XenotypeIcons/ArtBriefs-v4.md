# Xenotype icon art briefs — fourth direction

These briefs replace the design direction in IconConcepts.md and the V1–V3 generation prompts. The earlier renders remain useful examples of failure modes, not shapes to average. This document proposes new artwork; it does not change mod assets.

## Acceptance criteria

These are the acceptance criteria for every render. The art directions below are suggestions for achieving them, not additional pass/fail requirements.

- Everything must be legible at native 32 x 32 px.
- Retain the primarily solid white style, with black contours and identifying marks on transparency.
- No shading or fine details.

| Xenotype | Required recognition at 32 px |
| --- | --- |
| Zeegee | Recognizably a spacer. |
| Omegabeaver | Recognizably a beaver. |
| Scaleborn | Recognizably reptilian, with horns. |
| Succuboid | Recognizably a pretty woman, with small horns. |

## Shared direction

Create four front-facing head emblems that belong beside the existing Xyl Xenos icons: primarily solid white faces or helmet shells, confident black contours and identifying marks, and transparent backgrounds. Use flat shapes with no shading or fine details. Use the current icon contact sheet as the primary style reference.

The first impression should establish the kind of person or creature represented. Recognition should come from a small coherent combination of meaningful forms: anatomy and expression for most of the set, or a defining spaceflight association for Zeegee. A unique outline is helpful but is not mandatory; do not distort a head simply to make its silhouette different. An ordinary human face can be correct for a human-derived xenotype.

Design and judge at 32 x 32 px. Match the existing set's apparent size and weight, preserving aspect ratio and comparable margins. Use generous eye openings, clear separations, and a few broad shapes. Omit gray modeling, gradients, texture, tiny pupils, individual hair strands, and fine expression lines. The 128 px source export must survive reduction to 32 px.

Noses and mouths are available when they improve the intended impression. They are not compulsory, but the previous blanket exclusion of them was unnecessarily restrictive. Do not try to include every gene or biographical detail. Use a single direct visual idea rather than accumulating badges and props. For Zeegee, a vacsuit helmet is the primary subject: it expresses the xenotype's spaceborn identity. The other three briefs retain faces as their primary subjects.

## Zeegee — a spaceborn human

**Intended impression:** A person whose natural home is aboard ships and habitats. A familiar vacsuit helmet should make spaceflight the immediate association, conveying the technical, spaceborn side of the xenotype without requiring exaggerated facial anatomy.

**Primary reference:** [RimWorld vacsuit helmet](../../Rimworld%20art/Heads/VacsuitHelmet.png), supplied by the user. Use its recognizable combination of a pale rounded shell, broad dark faceplate, and substantial lower rim. Translate its construction into the simpler graphic language of the existing xenotype icons.

**Draw:** A centered front-facing vacsuit helmet, without shoulders or a suit torso. Build it from three dominant shapes: a rounded white upper shell, a broad solid black visor with softly rounded lower corners, and a white lower sealing rim that cups the visor. Use a confident black outer contour. Keep the silhouette compact and recognizably wearable by a human, with a little room around the head rather than the proportions of a skull or robot face.

Simplify the reference's side fittings into two small integrated blocks and its crown panel into one broad contour change only if these help retain the reference's identity at 32 px. The visor opening and surrounding shell should do most of the work. Omit vents, seams, bolts, tiny crown lines, glass reflections, shading, and mechanical greebles.

Keep the visor opaque. Do not cram eyes, a face, hair, and a forehead mark into the remaining area, or turn the biological forehead mark into a helmet decal. This concept deliberately represents the xenotype through its spaceborn association; it does not need to show its facial phenotype as well.

**Visual priority:** Readable vacsuit helmet first; recognizable RimWorld construction second. Preserve the rounded pressure shell and lower seal rather than emphasizing tactical armor or a narrow threatening eye slit.

**Guard against:** A motorcycle helmet, medieval visor, combat robot, or generic armored soldier. Avoid an aggressive brow, respirator grille, antenna, star badge, or extraneous space symbols. The overall impression should be practical space equipment.

**Acceptance at 32 px:** Must be recognizably a spacer.

## Omegabeaver — a sturdy, good-natured builder

**Intended impression:** A friendly, practical beaver person with useful strength in its jaws. Approachable and industrious, with enough substance to seem capable of building a dam. Its animal ancestry should be unmistakable.

**Draw:** A broad, compact beaver head with a relatively low crown and two modest round ears partly nestled into the upper sides. Restore the ears as functional animal anatomy, without making them large circular ornaments. Give the lower face a coherent, broad muzzle; its volume should replace the inflated hamster cheeks of earlier versions.

Make a blunt black beaver nose the central anchor. Beneath it, use a small mouth opening containing two medium chisel-shaped incisors, with a clear split and relatively flat cutting edges. The nose, muzzle, and teeth must read as one working anatomical unit. Teeth should be noticeable but occupy a modest portion of the face, with a solid lower jaw supporting them. Avoid a wide grinning mouth slot. Use small, level, open eyes for an easygoing expression. No whiskers or fur strokes are needed.

**Visual priority:** Beaver muzzle, nose, and paired teeth first; round ears and steady, friendly expression second. A broad grounded face should convey more practical substance than a balloon-shaped mascot.

**Guard against:** A human baby with buck teeth, a hamster, a plush bear, or a foolish cartoon rodent. Kindness and slow learning do not require an idiotic expression. Avoid dangling teeth, puffy cheeks, and a toothy comedy grin.

**Acceptance at 32 px:** Must be recognizably a beaver.

## Scaleborn — an armored dragonkin hunter

**Intended impression:** A powerful, composed reptilian hunter. The face should suggest tough scales, bodily weight, and confidence in its strength. Ferocity should feel available, rather than expressed as a permanent tantrum.

**Draw:** A broad head with a substantial brow, integrated cheek ridges, and a short strong jaw ending in a blunt chin. Build a continuous contour with a few anatomical changes of direction; no ornamental spikes or serrated cheek edges. Make the reptilian anatomy clear through the overall head shape, brow, and jaw. A short, broad muzzle is available if it improves recognition.

Grow two substantial horns from the temples, each with a broad base and one clean upward/backward sweep. Their scale should suggest real weapons without shrinking the face beneath them. A broad forehead scute or brow ridge may help establish reptilian anatomy. If used, render it as a solid white form with a simple black separation. Keep only large, readable structures; do not add gray modeling or tiny scales.

Place compact, slightly hooded eyes beneath the brow, with relatively level upper edges. A measured, assessing gaze should provide predatory intent without severely inward-slanting angry eyebrows. Leave the mouth closed and simple or unmarked.

**Visual priority:** Clearly reptilian head and visible horns. Strength and composure are supporting qualities. Keep the design neutral between lineages.

**Guard against:** A generic devil, an angry emoji, a dragon skull, or an aggressive sports-team logo. Avoid enormous horns on a small face, repeated zigzags, flames, and miscellaneous rock patches resembling Titan.

**Acceptance at 32 px:** Must be recognizably reptilian, with horns.

## Succuboid — an inviting, self-possessed companion

**Intended impression:** An attractive adult human woman whose attention feels warm, assured, and a little mischievous. The demonic traits should belong naturally to someone designed for pleasure and companionship. Youthful appearance is appropriate; childishness is not. Avoid making hostility or deceit the defining expression.

**Draw:** A softly sculpted human face with clear cheek shape and a small rounded chin. Frame it with long, well-kept hair rendered in two or three broad masses. Let the hair fall beside the face with a gentle sweep and modest ends, rather than the heavy bell-shaped flare of earlier renders. A simple slightly off-center part can give it an individual, cared-for appearance without adding strands or ornaments.

Place two small, rounded-base horns above the temples, integrated into the hair. Use relaxed, moderately wide eyes with soft lower curves and nearly level upper edges. Their openness and spacing should suggest easy social attention. Avoid dramatically pointed outer corners and sharply slanting inner corners; those created the alien or hostile reading. Do not rely on tiny eyelid lines or eyelashes to create expression.

A simple smile is optional if it helps the face read as pretty and remains a clear, bold mark at 32 px. Do not add fine mouth detail to pursue a subtle expression. Keep the cheeks and jaw mature enough that the smile does not turn the face into a cheerful child's doll. Leave the nose unmarked.

**Visual priority:** A recognizably pretty woman's face with visible small horns. Warmth and confidence are supporting qualities. Use face shape, eyes, and flowing hair together to establish the impression. Wings and tail can remain outside this head-only emblem.

**Guard against:** An alien mask, a cat woman, a porcelain doll, a sneering villain, or a passive decorative object. Do not interpret the lore's hostile societal judgments as a requirement to make every succuboid look predatory. Avoid heart symbols, lips as a separate motif, and cosmetic micro-detail.

**Acceptance at 32 px:** Must be recognizably a pretty woman, with small horns.

## Review the next renders

View each icon at native 32 px without its label and judge the required recognition in the acceptance table. Check that the relevant identity and horns, where required, are readable at that size.

Check the primarily solid white style and absence of shading or fine details. Compare alongside the current icons for visual consistency. Enlarged views can help diagnose a failure but cannot establish legibility at 32 px.

The descriptive moods, anatomical suggestions, and reference details in these briefs guide iteration. Do not turn them into extra acceptance tests, require hidden-feature exercises, or reject an otherwise successful icon because it omits an optional detail.
